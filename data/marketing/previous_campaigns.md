# Previous Campaigns — Northbrook Outfitters (Internal Summaries)

These are internal post-mortems for past (fictional) campaigns, kept
here so future campaign planning can reference what worked and what
didn't. Figures are illustrative demo values only.

## "Trail Ready" — Spring Acquisition Campaign (targeted Segment A)

- **Objective:** Acquisition — new customers ahead of hiking season.
- **Channels used:** Paid social (Instagram/Facebook) + paid search.
- **Key message:** "Everything you need for your first ten hikes,
  explained simply."
- **Internal review outcome:** Marked as a success internally. Creative
  emphasized approachability over technical specs, which matched
  Segment A's stated motivations. Paid search terms focused on
  beginner-intent queries ("what do I need for a day hike") rather
  than product-name queries, which internal review noted drove
  higher-quality new-customer signups than prior beginner campaigns.
- **Lesson carried forward:** For Segment A, beginner-intent search
  terms and multi-use framing ("wear it on the trail or around town")
  outperform pure product-spec messaging.

## "Gear That Lasts" — Fall Retention Campaign (targeted Segment B)

- **Objective:** Retention — re-engage existing Trail Veteran customers
  around fall gear refresh season.
- **Channels used:** Email + long-form blog content.
- **Key message:** "The gear you already trust, built to be repaired,
  not replaced."
- **Internal review outcome:** Marked as a success. Leaned into the
  lifetime repair program messaging pillar; email click-through was
  internally noted as notably higher than the brand's typical
  retention-email baseline for this segment.
- **Lesson carried forward:** Repair/durability messaging resonates
  strongly with Segment B specifically — has not been tested with
  other segments and should not be assumed to generalize to them.

## "Big Weekend Sale" — Reactivation Campaign (targeted Segment D, lapsed subset)

- **Objective:** Reactivation of lapsed loyalty members via a
  broad discount push.
- **Channels used:** Email (high frequency, 4 emails in 7 days) + paid
  social.
- **Key message:** Generic percentage-off messaging, no personalization.
- **Internal review outcome:** Marked as underperformed. Post-campaign
  review attributed this to two issues: (1) message mismatch — Segment
  D historically responds better to recognition/early-access framing
  than blanket discounts, and (2) email frequency was flagged as a
  likely driver of an elevated unsubscribe rate during the campaign
  window.
- **Lesson carried forward:** Avoid generic high-frequency discount
  blasts for Segment D; prior "Gear That Lasts"-style repair/loyalty
  framing has performed better for retention-oriented sends to this
  group.

## "Give the Trail" — Holiday Gift Campaign (targeted Segment C)

- **Objective:** Acquisition/awareness during the November-December
  gifting window.
- **Channels used:** Paid search (gift-intent terms) + in-store signage
  + simplified gift-guide landing page.
- **Key message:** "Not sure what they need? We'll help you pick."
- **Internal review outcome:** Marked as a success. The gift-guide
  format organized by recipient type (rather than by product category)
  was called out specifically in internal review as reducing decision
  friction for non-outdoorsy gift buyers.
- **Lesson carried forward:** For Segment C, organize gift
  recommendations by "who you're buying for," not by product taxonomy.
