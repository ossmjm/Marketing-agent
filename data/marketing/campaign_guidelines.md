# Campaign Guidelines — Northbrook Outfitters

Internal reference for planning marketing campaigns, covering approval
thresholds, channel mix defaults, and timing constraints.

## Standard Campaign Structure

Every campaign brief should define:
1. A single primary objective (awareness, acquisition, retention, or
   reactivation) — campaigns with more than one primary objective
   consistently underperform in post-campaign review, per internal
   retrospectives.
2. One primary target segment, with secondary segments noted only if
   the creative genuinely works for both without dilution.
3. A key message stated in one sentence.

## Channel Mix Defaults

- **Awareness campaigns:** Lead with Instagram and out-of-home
  (regional print/local partnerships); email plays a supporting role
  only.
- **Acquisition campaigns:** Lead with paid search and paid social;
  retarget site visitors via email if they've opted in.
- **Retention/loyalty campaigns:** Lead with email and app push;
  paid channels are rarely cost-effective for this objective given our
  existing-customer base.
- **Reactivation campaigns (lapsed customers):** Email first, with a
  single well-timed paid social touch; avoid over-emailing lapsed
  customers, which internal unsubscribe-rate tracking has linked to
  further disengagement.

## Timing Constraints

- Do not launch acquisition campaigns during the two weeks around
  major end-of-season clearance events — they cannibalize each other's
  budget efficiency.
- Gift & Occasion campaigns (Segment C) should launch no later than
  10 days before the relevant gifting deadline to allow for shipping
  cutoffs.
- Loyalty program campaigns perform best on a monthly cadence rather
  than one-off blasts, per internal engagement review notes.

## Approval Gates

- Any campaign with a stated budget must have that budget confirmed by
  Finance before external commitments (e.g. paid media bookings) are
  made — Marketing planning documents alone are not sufficient
  authorization.
- Campaigns referencing specific delivery timelines or specific
  discount amounts require Operations sign-off (see brand_guidelines.md
  "Prohibited Claims").

## Post-Campaign Review

Every campaign is reviewed against its stated KPIs 30 days after
launch. Common reasons past campaigns have been marked "underperformed"
in internal review (see previous_campaigns.md for specific examples):
- Mismatched channel and segment (e.g. heavy paid-search spend aimed
  at Segment D, who respond better to owned channels)
- Message tried to serve two objectives at once
- Insufficient distinction between the campaign's factual claims and
  its aspirational/creative claims, leading to legal/compliance review
  delays
