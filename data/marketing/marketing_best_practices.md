# Marketing Best Practices — Internal Reference

General principles the Northbrook marketing team applies when planning
campaigns, independent of any single past campaign.

## Message-Market Fit

A campaign's key message should map to ONE segment's primary
motivation, not attempt to satisfy every segment's motivations at
once. Trying to speak to beginners and technical veterans in the same
piece of creative tends to under-serve both — this is echoed in
Northbrook's own internal review notes (see previous_campaigns.md).

## Channel Selection Principles

- Paid channels are generally better suited to awareness/acquisition,
  where you're reaching people who don't yet know they want your
  product.
- Owned channels (email, app, loyalty program) are generally better
  suited to retention and reactivation, where the cost of a paid
  impression is hard to justify against an audience you can already
  reach directly.
- Frequency matters more than reach for retention audiences — but only
  up to a point; excessive frequency to an already-engaged list is a
  common cause of unsubscribe spikes.

## Objective Clarity

Every campaign should be able to answer: "if this works, what number
moves?" A campaign KPI should be a metric the team can actually
measure and attribute to the campaign (e.g. new email signups from a
specific landing page), not a vague aspiration ("build brand love").

## Claims and Compliance

Marketing copy should maintain a clear separation between:
- **Sourced facts** — things that come from verified operational data
  (inventory, pricing, delivery times, sustainability metrics).
- **Creative/aspirational language** — brand voice, evocative
  description, calls to action.

Blurring the two (e.g. stating a specific delivery promise as brand
voice rather than a sourced operational fact) is a common source of
both customer complaints and compliance review delays.

## Segmentation Discipline

Persona-based segments (like Northbrook's Segments A-D) are directional
targeting tools, not deterministic rules about individual customers.
Best practice is to use them to guide channel and message choices, not
to make absolute claims about what "all" members of a segment want.

## Testing and Iteration

Where possible, campaigns should include a defined way to learn
something for next time (e.g. testing two subject lines, or two
landing page framings) rather than shipping a single untested version
at full budget.
